# Apparatus Fleet Tracking

*Assets → Apparatuses* lets officers track all fire department vehicles, response trucks, pump ratings, and service schedules in one organized place.

## Apparatus categories and types

Every vehicle in your station fleet can be recorded with its specific type and mission:

| Vehicle Type | Common examples | Primary mission |
|---|---|---|
| **Engine / Pumper** | Engine 1, Pumper 12 | Carries hose, water, and a high-capacity fire pump |
| **Ladder / Tower** | Ladder 3, Tower 1 | Aerial ladders, elevated master streams, and roof ventilation |
| **Heavy Rescue** | Rescue 1, Squad 2 | Vehicle extrication tools, search equipment, and technical rescue |
| **Brush Truck** | Brush 4, Old Brush Truck | Off-road 4x4 response for wildland, woods, and field fires |
| **Tanker / Tender** | Tanker 2, Tender 5 | Transports large volumes of water to rural areas without fire hydrants |
| **Ambulance / Medic** | Medic 1, EMS Squad 3 | Emergency medical care, patient stabilization, and hospital transport |
| **Command / Utility** | Chief 1, Car 2, Utility 5 | Incident command, support equipment, and personnel transport |

## Operational status

Every apparatus displays a color-coded status badge so dispatchers and officers know which trucks can respond immediately:

- **In Service (Green)**: Ready for emergency calls. The vehicle is fully staffed, stocked with equipment, and mechanically sound.
- **In Shop / Maintenance (Amber)**: The vehicle is undergoing scheduled maintenance, oil changes, or non-emergency repairs.
- **Out of Service (Red)**: The vehicle is not available for emergency calls due to mechanical failure, damage, or urgent safety issues.
- **Reserve (Blue)**: A backup apparatus kept in reserve to replace a front-line truck when it goes in for service.
- **Retired (Gray)**: A decommissioned, historical, or sold vehicle kept for historical department records.

## Tracking pump and tank capabilities

Fire trucks rely on pumps and water tanks to fight fires safely. You can track exact pump capacities (such as 1,500 GPM) and onboard water tank volumes (such as 750 Gallons) for every engine, tanker, and brush unit.

## Maintenance and pump testing

Keeping fire trucks reliable protects both firefighters and the community:

- **Next Service Due**: Record the date of upcoming routine service or state vehicle inspections. The system automatically highlights dates that are coming up soon or overdue.
- **Annual Pump Certification**: Record the date of the last annual fire pump flow test to ensure pumps operate at full rated capacity.
- **Mileage and Engine Hours**: Keep track of vehicle odometer readings and pump engine run hours over time.

> [!NOTE]
> Existing apparatus records in your database (such as Old Brush Truck) appear automatically and can be edited anytime to add unit numbers, pump ratings, or service dates.
